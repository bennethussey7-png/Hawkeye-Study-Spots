How to plan a productive group study session

Studying with classmates can make challenging assignments feel more manageable, especially when everyone arrives prepared. Choosing the right space and setting clear expectations can help your group make the most of its time together.

Choose a room that fits your group

Before meeting, think about how many people will attend and what activities you plan to complete. A smaller room may work well for reviewing lecture notes or asking each other practice questions. A larger collaborative space may be more useful for group projects, presentations, or assignments that require multiple laptops and textbooks. Consider whether everyone will have enough seating and table space to participate comfortably.

Check reservation rules and equipment

Some university study spaces may require reservations, while others may operate on a first-come, first-served basis. Always check official University of Iowa resources for current policies, building access, and room availability. If your group needs a screen, whiteboard, electrical outlets, or other equipment, confirm those features before meeting. Set a goal before your group meets

Group study sessions are usually more productive when everyone understands what needs to be accomplished. Instead of meeting without a plan, decide whether your goal is to prepare for an exam, finish a project, or review difficult course concepts. You might divide the session into smaller sections, such as reviewing notes, answering practice questions, and discussing topics that are still confusing. Having a simple agenda can keep conversations focused.

Make sure everyone participates

One advantage of studying in a group is hearing different explanations and perspectives. Encourage each person to contribute by taking turns explaining concepts or asking questions. For exam preparation, group members can create practice questions and quiz one another. For collaborative assignments, assigning responsibilities can help prevent one person from doing most of the work. Respectful communication is important, especially when classmates have different schedules or study habits.

Keep distractions under control

Even in a designated study room, it can be easy for conversations to shift away from coursework. Consider putting phones on silent and agreeing on short breaks during longer sessions. If your group becomes distracted, return to the agenda and identify the next task. It can also help to finish each meeting by reviewing what was accomplished and deciding what everyone should complete before the next session.

Group study checklist

Before your session, confirm the meeting time, group size, location, room rules, needed materials, and study goals. A little preparation can save everyone time.
