University of Iowa • Student study guide

Make every study session count.

A productive study session starts with the right environment, realistic goals, and a plan that fits your schedule.

These are general study suggestions, not live information about campus facilities.

Find a study routine that works for you

There is no single study strategy that works for every student. The best approach depends on the assignment, your energy level, and the environment around you.

Focus strategy

Set one clear goal

Before opening your laptop or notebook, decide what you want to accomplish. Instead of planning to study for an entire afternoon without direction, choose a specific goal such as reviewing lecture notes, outlining an essay, or completing practice problems. Clear goals make it easier to measure progress and stay motivated.

Time management

Break work into manageable sessions

Long study sessions can feel overwhelming, especially when several assignments are due close together. Consider working in focused blocks with short breaks between them. During each block, concentrate on one task instead of switching constantly between homework, messages, and social media. Adjust the length of each session to match your attention span.

Study environment

Match your space to your assignment

Different types of work benefit from different surroundings. Reading and writing may be easier in a quiet area, while collaborative projects often require a space where conversation is welcome. Think about noise, seating, lighting, and access to outlets before deciding where to study. Explore our Quiet Spaces guide for additional ideas.

Plan before you leave

Bring the materials you need, check available campus resources, and decide how long you want to work. Preparing ahead of time can reduce interruptions once your study session begins.

Make studying more effective

Choosing a study location is only the beginning. How you use your time matters just as much as where you spend it.

Learning strategy

Practice active recall

Instead of repeatedly rereading the same notes, try testing yourself on the material. Close your notebook and explain an important concept from memory, answer practice questions, or create flashcards. This approach helps you identify what you understand and what needs more attention before an exam.

Group work

Use group study time intentionally

Studying with classmates can help when you need to compare ideas, discuss difficult concepts, or prepare a presentation. However, group sessions are most useful when everyone understands the goal. Agree on what you want to accomplish before meeting, and choose a location where conversation is appropriate. Visit our Study Rooms guide for planning suggestions.

Healthy habits

Recognize when you need a break

Studying for hours without rest does not always lead to better learning. If you notice that you are rereading the same paragraph or losing concentration, take a short break and return with a clearer mind. Staying hydrated, eating regularly, and getting adequate sleep can also support a more productive academic routine.

Explore your options

Your ideal study environment may change throughout the semester. Browse our Campus Areas guide to think about which parts of campus fit your schedule and study preferences.
